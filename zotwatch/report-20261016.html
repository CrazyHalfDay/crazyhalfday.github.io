Inherited lithospheric weakness enabled rapid stress transmission across Tibet

2026-10-15
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Zhaotong Sun，Giulio Viola，Leonardo Del Sole，Jing Liu-Zeng，Yong Zheng 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78650-2

K–Ar dating reveals >110 Myr of episodic Altyn Tagh Fault activity, beginning at ~115 Ma along a Cretaceous lithospheric weakness. Reactivation at ~56 Ma records rapid India–Asia collisional stress transmission >1500 km into the continental interior. The rate and efficiency of stress transmission across continental interiors during collision remain poorly constrained. The >1600-km-long Altyn Tagh Fault, bounding the northern Tibetan Plateau, offers a key test of stress propagation models. Here, we integrate multiscale structural analysis with K–Ar dating of synkinematic illite to date its faulting history. The data document episodic faulting spanning >110 million years, including an ~115 Ma event marking a lithospheric weakness zone predating Cenozoic collision, and an Early Eocene faulting age (~56 Ma), which is nearly synchronous with India–Asia collision onset, implying rapid far-field stress transmission across distances exceeding 1500 km within 10 million years of collision initiation. Reactivation at ~26 Ma, ~2.7 Ma and ~0.6 Ma reflects localization during plateau growth. These results show that inherited lithospheric structures enable efficient, long-distance stress transmission and remain persistent deformation loci, indicating that continental interiors respond to plate-boundary forcing faster than slow, progressive propagation models predict.

Nickel isotopes in Apollo and Chang’e soils reveal widespread micrometeorite-driven processing of the lunar regolith

2026-10-15
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Ke Zhu，Maosheng Yang，Jun Huang，Wei Tian，Meng-Hua Zhu 等

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04139-x

Abstract Micrometeorite bombardment is a fundamental surface process on the Moon and other airless bodies, driving elemental evaporation, isotopic fractionation, and material mixing in surface regoliths. Here, we report high-precision stable Ni isotope data for lunar soils returned by the Apollo (11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17) and Chang’e (5 and 6) missions, covering geographically and geologically diverse lunar terrains. The analyzed soils show δ 60/58 Ni values of 0.345‰ to 1.280‰ (relative to NIST SRM 986), generally heavier than lunar basalts and the inferred bulk silicate Moon, indicating widespread heavy Ni isotope enrichment during surface processing. Together with published Chang’e-5 drill-core data, these results demonstrate that micrometeorite-driven Ni isotope fractionation is widespread across the lunar surface. Local variability, however, reflects additional controls. The relatively low δ 60/58 Ni values of the immature Apollo 12 soils are consistent with limited cumulative micrometeorite processing, whereas the mature Apollo 16 soils have high Ni concentrations and Ni/Co ratios, indicating additional compositional overprinting by Ni-rich material. Stable Ni isotopes, particularly when combined with regolith maturity and Ni/Co ratios, therefore provide a sensitive tracer of micrometeorite-driven lunar surface processing and its local overprinting.

Archean subduction-derived noble gases in the lithospheric mantle beneath the São Francisco Craton

2026-10-15
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Luisa Gomes Braga，Tiago Jalowitzki，Hirochika Sumino，Reinhardt Adolfo Fuck，Eduardo Balbinot 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78667-7

While mid-ocean ridge basalts (MORB) and ocean island basalts (OIB) are well characterized in terms of helium, neon, argon, and xenon isotopes, the continental lithosphere, especially cratonic domains and subduction settings, remain poorly constrained. Here, we report noble gas (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, and Xe) isotope ratios in peridotite, pyroxenite, and eclogite xenoliths from the SW margin of the São Francisco Craton, Brazil. Helium isotopes reveal a strong radiogenic component, while neon shows MORB-like and nucleogenic signatures. This divergence indicates the recycling of subduction-derived materials within the stratified cratonic mantle (2.0–5.5 GPa). Xenon reveals a cratonic lithosphere where air and fissiogenic (crustal) components variably mix with a MORB source. The recycling of ancient hydrated oceanic crust and overlying sediments, characterized by high (U+Th)/(^3He, ^22Ne, ^132Xe) ratios, is associated with a complex and long-lived subduction system established along the SW margin of the São Francisco Craton since the Archean. This study reveals that noble gas isotopes in mantle rocks beneath the São Francisco Craton, Brazil, record the recycling of volatiles from Archean subduction into the continental mantle.

Active crustal straining and fault reactivation at Valles Marineris

2026-10-15
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Jung-Hun Song，Doyeon Kim，Folarin Kolawole，Joel M. Davis，Rebekah Parry 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78193-6

Abstract Tectonic deformation on stagnant-lid planets reflects the interaction between long-lived lithospheric structures and evolving stress fields. Whether the ancient tectonic fabrics can localise present-day seismic failure under contemporary stress fields remains poorly tested by direct geophysical observations. Here we show that the farside seismic event, S0976a, detected by InSight in the western hemisphere is a marsquake that occurred in Valles Marineris and involved strike-slip faulting in the deep crust. The stress orientation inferred from the focal mechanism is consistent with reactivation of pre-existing fault systems under a present-day stress field shaped primarily by long-term planetary cooling, regional Tharsis-related loading, and surface erosion-related local unloading. Surface carbonation governs olivine reactivity for rapid CO2 mineralization

2026-10-15
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Zihua Shao，Yunfeng Liang，Gyuhwan Jo，Keisuke Nakamura，Takeshi Tsuji

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78548-z

Abstract Ultramafic rocks are a global resource with vast potential for permanent CO 2 mineralization. However, the origin of the striking reactivity difference between Ca- and Mg-bearing olivine remains poorly understood. Here we show that this difference arises not merely from the type of cation, but more fundamentally from how these cations modulate the oxygen reactivity. Nonbridging oxygens serve as the oxygen source for CO 2 surface reactions, which in Ca-olivine reduce the Ca 2+ dissolution barrier by 60% and accelerate ion release by six orders of magnitude. The calculated apparent dissolution rate constants reproduce the experimentally observed complete conversion of Ca-olivine within hours under 1 atm CO 2 . This multiscale framework reveals how surface reactions govern silicate carbonation and provides a predictive basis for modeling and engineering large-scale CO 2 mineralization in both synthetic materials and basaltic reservoirs.

On predictability of slip, rupture geometry, and speed of the 2025 M7.8 Mandalay, Myanmar, earthquake

2026-10-14
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Thomas Ulrich，Xiaoyu Zou，Mathilde Marchandon，Nico Schliwa，Fengzhou Tan 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-77203-x

Abstract The 2025 M 7.8 Mandalay earthquake ruptured ~480 km of the Sagaing Fault, producing one of the longest strike-slip events documented and impacting a vulnerable region. Here we combine remote sensing observations and numerical modeling to show that the subsurface fault geometry and slip distribution could be anticipated from pre-event observations. Along-strike variations in coseismic and interseismic deformation reveal a helix-shaped fault geometry. The average coseismic slip equals or exceeds the interseismic slip deficit, consistent with a minimally slip-predictable model. We generate an ensemble of 3D dynamic rupture models, constrained by the best-fit static slip model, then discriminate among them using independent space-geodetic, seismic, and near-field video observations. The preferred scenario begins bilaterally, then transitions to unilateral southward supershear propagation at depth, while shallow rupture remains subshear. These dynamics are shaped by localized stress heterogeneity and low fracture energy, characteristic of a structurally mature fault zone. Our approach can inform rupture scenarios in understudied regions.

Deep hydrous melting of the mantle wedge recorded by Mg isotopes

2026-10-14

Winnie X. Fan，Fang-Zhen Teng，Yinbiao Peng，Anna D. Berg，William P. Leeman

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aei6572

Subduction zones govern Earth’s deep volatile cycle. The slab-mantle interface is a key locus of volatile exchange between the subducting crust and the overlying mantle wedge, yet whether and how chemical reactions at this interface affect arc magmatism remains poorly constrained. Arc magmas typically lack a geochemical hallmark of garnet fractionation, leading to the prevailing interpretation that they derive predominantly from the shallower, garnet-free mantle wedge. Monte Carlo isotope mixing models indicate that up to 50% of the Mg budget in some arc magma sources derives from deep, water-saturated melting near the slab-mantle interface. These findings highlight the base of the mantle wedge as a volumetrically and geochemically significant contributor to arc magmatism and the water cycle.

Abstract Dark colored grassland soils, known as Mollisols, are intensively farmed and rich in organic matter, and hence have an important role in the carbon cycle. Phylogenomics confirms Palaeoptera monophyly and illuminates the origin of winged insects

2026-10-12
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Shiyu Du，Yehao Wang，Feng Zhang，Michael S. Engel，Xuhongyi Zheng 等

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2614706123

The origin and early radiation of winged insects (Pterygota) represent one of the most transformative events in terrestrial evolution, yet phylogenetic relationships among their basalmost lineages (Ephemeroptera, Odonata and Neoptera) continue to be a central debate within insect systematics. Here, we present the most taxonomically comprehensive phylogenomic analysis to date, leveraging genomic and transcriptomic data from 101 species, including 16 sequenced ephemeropteran genomes. Employing 1,367 universal single-copy orthologs across two supermatrices, we used concatenation- and coalescent-based frameworks under homogeneous and heterogeneous, and multispecies coalescent models. Our analyses support Palaeoptera monophyly. Although coalescent-based methods initially suggested a Chiastomyaria (Ephemeroptera + Neoptera) topology, subsequent signal dissection and topology tests confirm that the preponderance of genomic signal favors a monophyletic Palaeoptera. Furthermore, we identify incomplete lineage sorting and gene tree estimation error as the primary drivers of previous topological conflicts. Our molecular dating results point to an Early Devonian origin of crown-Pterygota, whereas crown-Palaeoptera first emerged in the Late Devonian. By reconciling molecular evidence with morphological synapomorphies, such as wing-base sclerite homologies, this study provides a coherent framework for the early evolution of winged insects, supports a single origin of wings, and identifies the neopteran wing-folding mechanism as a singular evolutionary innovation.

Physics-informed deep learning links geodetic data and fault friction

物理信息深度学习将大地测量数据与断层摩擦联系起来

2026-10-12
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Rikuto Fukushima，Masayuki Kano，Kazuro Hirahara，Makiko Ohtani

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04138-y

Fault slip modeling, based on laboratory-derived friction laws, has significantly enhanced our understanding of fault mechanics. Agreement between model predictions and observations supports the hypothesis that observed slip diversity, including fast earthquakes and slow transient slips (Slow Slip Events; SSEs), originates from frictional heterogeneity. However, quantitative assessments of frictional heterogeneity from geodetic observations while fully incorporating fault mechanics are lacking due to the difficulties of high-dimensional optimization. In this study, we aim to address this gap using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to link frictional heterogeneity with geodetic observations. PINNs employ a neural network to represent the spatially variable frictional properties, making their estimation feasible. Targeting the 2010 Bungo SSE in southwest Japan, our estimation reveals heterogeneous friction coinciding with localized SSE nucleation in southwest Shikoku, and subsequent westward propagation. The calculated fault slip of SSE successfully reproduces the spatio-temporal pattern of observed surface displacements. This PINN-based inversion provides a mechanically consistent fault slip model validated through quantitative comparison with observations. Furthermore, we forecast the future fault slip evolution, demonstrating the importance of assimilating observations spanning multiple SSE cycles. Our results demonstrate the potential of PINN for advancing understanding of fault mechanics and enabling physics-based fault slip forecasting. Spatially variable frictional properties along a fault can be estimated using a physics-informed neural network approach that enables physics-consistent slip modelling, as tested on the 2010 Bungo slow slip event in southwest Japan.

Earth and Planetary Science Letters (IF: 4.8)

Ming Tang，Zi Ye，Xu Chu

DOI: 10.1016/j.epsl.2026.120409

S-type granites are far less abundant during much of the Archean than in the Phanerozoic, and this paucity remains puzzling. We propose that the suppression of S-type granite formation at scale may have been an inherent consequence of the hotter Archean crust. A key insight from fossil P-T-t paths in representative orogens is that the onset of melting can trigger exhumation and thereby limit the maximum burial depth of sediments.​ In a hot crust, this maximum burial depth is relatively shallow, because hot geotherms initiate melting at shallow depths due to the pressure dependence of dehydration melting. Such low melt productivity in the Archean may have inhibited the formation of large S-type plutons and batholiths. Guo Jing，Liang Sheng-xian，Jiao Yan-jie

DOI: 10.1016/j.oregeorev.2026.107600

Since the India-Eurasia continental collision, the Tethyan Himalaya has undergone distinctive tectono-magmatic events, including the emplacement of leucogranites and the development of the South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS). These processes have generated a series of Pb-Zn-Sb-Au deposits that are closely tied to tectono-magmatic activity. However, the spatiotemporal relationships between structures, magmatism, and mineralization, as well as the pathways for fluid migration, remain poorly constrained. This gap in understanding has impeded both the development of a robust metallogenic theory and the deployment of effective exploration strategies. We integrate multi-scale geophysical surveys, carbonaceous Raman spectroscopy thermometry, and 40Ar-39Ar geochronology to achieve the following objectives: (1) construct a comprehensive spatiotemporal framework linking the deposit to regional structures and magmatism; (2) delineate the ore-forming fluid migration pathways; and (3) propose a new metallogenic model and exploration approach. Our results demonstrate that:(1) A low-resistivity, low-density, partially molten layer exists at a depth of 10–15 km beneath Zhaxikang. (2) A multi-scale fault network (F1-2 throw ≈ 1.67 km, F2-2 throw ≈ 1.44 km, F3-1 throw ≈ 328 m, V ore vein throw ≈ 172–326 m) controls long-distance, multi-stage fluid migration.
