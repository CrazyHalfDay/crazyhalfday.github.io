Onset of zircon decomposition during saprolitic weathering

2026-10-10
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Martin Yan Hei Li，Mei-Fu Zhou

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04140-4

Abstract Zircon is conventionally considered as refractory in supergene environments, enabling the preservation of geochemical records for tracing various Earth’s processes and the use of the zircon structure as a nuclear waste form. However, how refractory zircon is during weathering, especially when crystalline and in an early stage of weathering, is unknown. In this study, we demonstrate that crystalline zircon would start to weather during an early-stage saprolitic weathering. Nano-scale examination reveals that weathering of zircon proceeds through dissolution of primary, crystalline zircon and precipitation of secondary, amorphous zircon. Non-formula elements, like U, Th and heavy rare earth elements, are uptake and structurally incorporated in this process. Thereby, metamictisation of the zircon would accelerate and eventually promote the dissolution of zircon. In supergene environments, zircon would steadily weather, calling for further evaluation on the geological and environmental applications of zircon.

Hydrogen isotopes reveal water leakage from the core

2026-10-09
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Yu Zhang，Wenzhong Wang，Zhongqing Wu，Bowen Chen

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aei4756

Chemical heterogeneities in Earth’s deep mantle may record either preserved primordial reservoirs or later core-mantle exchange, but distinguishing between these origins remains challenging. Hydrogen isotopes offer a sensitive tracer, yet whether magma ocean crystallization could generate a deuterium-depleted deep reservoir has not been quantitatively evaluated. Here, we use machine learning–accelerated path-integral simulations to determine equilibrium hydrogen isotope fractionation between silicate melt and bridgmanite, ringwoodite, and wadsleyite under magma ocean conditions. Incorporating these fractionation factors into a magma ocean crystallization model shows that mineral-melt fractionation is intrinsically weak, producing an essentially homogeneous D/H distribution in the primitive mantle. Magma ocean crystallization therefore cannot generate the extremely low δD values observed in some ocean island basalts. Combined with exceptionally high 3 He/ 4 He ratios, these signatures more plausibly reflect selective transfer of primordial volatiles from Earth’s core, implying sustained volatile exchange across the core-mantle boundary.

The riverine land-to-ocean carbon flux

2026-10-09
Nature Reviews Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 37.2)

Robert G. M. Spencer，Travis W. Drake，Maodian Liu，Peter A. Raymond

DOI: 10.1038/s43017-026-00834-7

The land-to-ocean export of carbon via rivers is a key component in the global carbon cycle. In this Review, we provide an overview of the factors governing the mobilization of carbon into rivers and those modulating riverine carbon export to the ocean. Terrestrial landscapes are estimated to deliver 3–4 PgC yr^−1 to inland waters, with the majority outgassed rather than exported to the ocean. Model estimates converge on a total global carbon export flux of 0.8–1.0 PgC yr^−1; however, regional, watershed and carbon species resolved estimates vary substantially owing to differences in source inputs, transport processes, biogeochemical transformations, and climate and hydrological drivers. Across the models that were synthesized, global estimates for individual carbon species span approximately a threefold difference, whereas estimated export of some species to the Atlantic and Pacific basins varies by up to fivefold. The Amazon and Congo together contribute approximately 21% of global river discharge, yet remain far less observed than many smaller temperate watersheds; the world’s 30 largest rivers by discharge account for approximately half of global cumulative river discharge. Therefore, expanding observations is clearly a priority to reduce uncertainties in modelled land-to-ocean carbon fluxes, and could be supported by the development of sensor networks and remote sensing observations to improve spatiotemporal resolution. Integrating these large datasets with mechanistic studies of biogeochemical modulators and machine learning approaches would inform understanding of carbon dynamics within major watersheds and improve predictions of this globally important carbon flux. Globally, rivers export ~10^15 grams of carbon to the ocean each year. This Review explores the mobilization, modulation and transport of different species of carbon within watersheds and rivers, with the aim to help inform and improve land-to-ocean carbon flux estimates.

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aei8075

The mechanism driving accelerating unrest at the densely populated Campi Flegrei caldera since 2005 remains debated. Analyzing an unprecedented two-decade geochemical dataset, we show that escalating soil CO 2 fluxes closely tracks caldera uplift, revealing magmatic fluid pressurization at 3- to 4-kilometer depth and heating as the fundamental driver of the unrest. A critical magmatic gas pulse occurred between 2018 and 2022 specifically focused this outgassing beneath the Solfatara crater. Thermodynamic modeling demonstrates that an increasing mass of ascending fluid pressurizes the hydrothermal system, forcing vapor condensation within the upper kilometer. Ultimately, this fluid pressurization and heating drive accelerating deformation and seismicity; alongside the 2021 emergence of seismic sequences with short inter-event times, it directly signals an escalating phreatic or magmatic eruption risk.

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aec1413

How mining has shaped long-term economic and political landscapes across Europe and the Mediterranean remains poorly understood, largely because archaeological evidence is spatially fragmented and ore sources are difficult to trace from atmospheric lead (Pb) records. 2026-10-05

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78212-6

Abstract Bridgmanite, the main mineralogical phase in the Earth’s lower mantle, is intrinsically anisotropic and may therefore generate seismic anisotropy in actively-deforming regions, particularly subduction zones. The bulk of the lower mantle is widely regarded as well-mixed but recent seismic observations suggest structures at mid-mantle depths whose origin remains unclear. Here, we propose a mechanism that could generate seismic discontinuities beneath subduction zones with depth distributions consistent with some of the observed mid-mantle reflectors. While our models predict ~ 1.5% contrast in fast versus slow shear wave velocities in terms of radial anisotropy, our full waveform simulations show that the discontinuity is detectable with array seismological methods. These results suggest that mid-mantle reflectors near subduction zones may be strain-induced, providing an additional mechanism that links mantle flow, mineral physics, and seismic observations.

2026-10-09

Hesen Zhao，Qingfei Wang，David I. Groves，Chang Yu，Richeng Zhong 等

DOI: 10.1016/j.epsl.2026.120387

Quartz-vein gold deposits dominate Earth’s gold endowment. However, the origin of their ore-forming fluids remains contested. While the traditional model invokes crustal metamorphism of silica-rich rocks, fluids derived from metasomatized mantle have emerged as a viable alternative. Yet how siliceous ore-forming fluids are released from this mantle source remains unclear. Linking mineralization styles to pyrite geochemistry: Implications for ore genesis and exploration in the Jiaojia gold deposit, Jiaodong Peninsula, China

2026-10-07

Wei Yang，Liang Zhang，Li-Qiang Yang，Lin-Nan Guo，Zheng-Jiang Ding 等

DOI: 10.1130/b38965.1

The giant Jiaojia gold deposit (&gt;200 t Au) in the Jiaodong Peninsula, China, hosts three distinct mineralization styles: disseminated, breccia, and stockwork (orebodies I and II); thin pyrite-muscovite-quartz veins (orebody III); and auriferous quartz-sulfide veins (orebody IV). These styles are spatially controlled by a multiorder fault network. Integrated in situ textural, trace-element, and sulfur isotope analyses of pyrite reveal that these styles formed during a single hydrothermal event, with gold precipitation mechanisms varying systematically with structural setting. These findings establish a genetic model directly linking specific ore styles to their structural domains, providing a predictive framework for exploration.

Mid-Miocene lithospheric mantle delamination and rejuvenation of Himalayan orogenesis

2026-10-07
Earth-Science Reviews (IF: 10.8)

Hongli Wang，Jinjiang Zhang，Kyle Larson，Jiamin Wang

DOI: 10.1016/j.earscirev.2026.105713

Continuous Cenozoic India-Asia convergence and collision has formed the Himalayan orogen with complex multiphase structural deformation, magmatism and metamorphism, preserving critical records for unraveling the Himalayan orogenic process and collisional orogeny. Following the intense shortening and thickening, the exhumation of the orogenic core (the Greater Himalayan Crystalline, GHC) and related stretching activities along the South Tibetan Detachment System (STDS) have occurred since the Oligocene. By the mid-Miocene, activities along the STDS ceased, while the N-S contractional structures, such as the Main Central Thrust (MCT), Gyirong-Kangma thrust (GKT), and Main Boundary Thrust (MBT), (re-) activated. Concurrently, extensive anatexis at multiple depths, diverse magmatism involving contributions from the Tethyan Himalayan Sequence (THS), GHC, Lesser Himalayan Sequence (LHS), Indian thickened lower crust, and lithospheric mantle, ultrahigh-temperature metamorphism, and the rapid uplift of the orogen occurred. The mid-Miocene deep processes, relationships among these tectono-anatexis-magmatic-metamorphic events, as well as implications for the Himalayan orogenesis remain poorly constrained. This paper reviews the records of tectonics, anatexis, magmatism, metamorphism and uplift history of typical localities in the orogen, with a focus on the mid-Miocene geological processes. It is concluded that the delamination of Indian lithospheric mantle triggered the (ultra-) high temperature anatexis-magmatic-metamorphic events and rapid uplift of the orogen in the mid-Miocene. The removal of the orogenic root reduced the resistance to underthrusting, induced the (re-) activation of the thrusts, and rejuvenated the Himalayan orogenesis, with its influence in Tibet and its adjacent regions. Undoubtedly, the continuous convergence between continents serves as the fundamental driving mechanism of the Himalayan orogenic rejuvenation.

New trace element data for chromitites from the mantle section of the Oman ophiolite: implications for chromite-melt trace element partitioning and the composition of parental magmas

2026-10-09
Lithos (IF: 4.5)

Hugh Rollinson，Davide Lenaz，Alberto Zanetti

DOI: 10.1016/j.lithos.2026.108814

We present new trace element data for chromitites from a six-km mantle section of the Oman ophiolite for samples with a range of compositions between high-alumina and high-chrome chromitites. We propose they represent the evolution of a single magmatic system, thus limiting the number of factors which influence the partitioning of trace elements into chromite. We explore relationships between the major element chemistry and trace elements in chromite, in order to better understand the crystal chemical controls on their partitioning, and make inferences about the nature of the magma parental to these chromitites. We also evaluate the extent to which late oxidation might influence the distribution of trace elements. We report inter-element relationships in chromite which are consistent with the preferential occupancy of divalent Mn, Co, Ni and Zn ions on the tetrahedral site, Ni partitioning governed by Mg concentrations, and Mn, Co, Zn partitioning by Fe2+ concentrations. However, some Ni partitioning is also governed by Cr concentrations on the octahedral site and Co and Zn concentrations are governed, in part, by Fe3+ concentrations on the octahedral site. Of the non-divalent elements Sc follows Cr on the octahedral site and Dchrt/melt Sc increases with increasing cr#; Ti, Ga and V concentrations correlated with Al concentrations also on the octahedral site. Dchrt/melt Ti increases whereas Dchrt/melt Ga decreases with increasing cr#. To a lesser extent Ga and V are also influenced by Fe3+ concentrations on the octahedral site. Calculated melt compositions indicate that the parental melts of both the low- and the high-cr# chromites were originally boninitic in composition and which interacted with the enclosing mantle harzburgite as they evolved. We propose that these melts represent a system of related, evolving magmas and that the high- and low-cr# chromites are part of the same magmatic system.
