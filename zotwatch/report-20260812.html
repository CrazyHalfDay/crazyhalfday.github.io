A missing continental impact melt sheet signal in the early Earth detrital zircon record

早期地球碎屑锆石记录中缺失的大陆撞击熔融席信号

2026-08-11
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Ian Szumila，Dustin Trail，Miki Nakajima，Veanessya Vazquez-Lopez，Rasmus Haugaard 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76529-w

Abstract Impact-derived melting has been proposed as a significant process in the formation of Earth’s early crust which could mean that impact melts are a source for Earth’s oldest minerals including ancient zircons. However, the source lithology of many Archean and Hadean zircons is unclear because many grains are detrital. Therefore, in this work, we develop a machine learning model trained on the U/Yb, Th/U, Yb, and Gd/Yb values of zircons from the 1.85 Ga Sudbury impact melt sheet and more typical igneous processes, so that it could distinguish between them. Here, we show that ancient zircons from three >3.2Ga localities did not crystallize from impact melt sheets. Our results support the hypothesis that early earth zircons are not sourced directly from impact melt sheets, especially one produced by impacting continental crust and indicate no evidence for a late heavy bombardment implying a more habitable Hadean.

Secular change in orogenic duration reveals alternating tectonic regimes during the Archaean-Proterozoic

2026-08-11

Lei Zou，Guangyu Huang，Jinghui Guo，Peter A. Cawood，Jia-Hui Liu 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76530-3

Abstract Geological constraints and modelling results reveal major yet poorly understood changes in Earth’s tectonic regimes during the late Archaean and Proterozoic. These changes are associated with supercontinent cycles, a primary archive of lithosphere and convecting mantle behaviour, and impact orogen duration and thermal state. However, the lack of precise data on orogen duration during Columbia assembly limits our understanding of the long-term tectonic evolution and comparison with younger orogens. Through in-situ garnet Lu–Hf and monazite microdomain U–Pb geochronology, here we constrain a 220 Myr hot collisional orogen during Columbia assembly, significantly longer than contemporary cold orogens (110–160 Myr). Secular changes in collisional orogen duration indicate a non-monotonic trend from Neoarchaean to Neoproterozoic. This trend implies enhanced plate rigidity and mobility during Columbia assembly, followed by a marked decline during Rodinia assembly, consistent with inferred plate velocity proxies. These findings support evolving tectonic regimes alternating between sluggish lid and more active plate tectonics during the Neoarchaean-Proterozoic.

Mechanoradical hydrogen generation during seismic slip in an active fault zone

活动断裂带地震滑动过程中机械自由基产氢

2026-08-10
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Yun Ya Ling，Li-Wei Kuo，Kuo-Fong Ma，Wen-Jie Wu

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03912-2

Black fault gouge identified in the rupture zone of the 2018 Hualien earthquake, Taiwan, bears abundant CaO and organic matter together with frictional melt, suggesting significant shear heating and natural hydrogen generation via mechanoradical reactions. Laboratory experiments and gas measurements suggest that rapid shearing in fault zones can trigger mechanoradical reactions that dissociate water and produce natural hydrogen. However, the lack of direct evidence means it is unclear whether this process operates in nature. We present mineralogical and microstructural observations of fault gouges recovered from the Milun Fault core three years after the 2018 M_W 6.4 Hualien earthquake in Taiwan. Quartz-rich yellow gouge contains clay and calcite but lacks carbonaceous material (CM). In contrast, black gouge contains crystallized frictional melt together with lime (CaO) and CM, suggesting that silicate minerals and calcite underwent shear heating during seismic slip. The formation of CM is experimentally associated with hydrogen-rich conditions, whereas lime is unstable in the presence of water. Therefore, the coexistence of lime and CM suggests formation under hydrogen-bearing and water-depleted conditions, consistent with mechanoradical reactions. Our results provide evidence of decarbonation and possible mechanoradical hydrogen generation during shearing in natural fault zones.

地球倒数第二次冰室期间的一次短暂全球变暖事件

Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Le Yao，Thomas J. Algeo，Qiulai Wang，Wang Zheng，Qiang Wei 等

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2601643123

Deep-time hyperthermal/transient warming events provide critical analogs for modern climatic warming and its impacts. However, all ancient hyperthermals documented to date have occurred under greenhouse climate states, limiting their relevance for understanding the trajectory of present-day climate change. Here, we present conodont oxygen isotope data and climate modeling for the Kasimovian–Gzhelian Thermal Maximum (KGTM) at ~304 Ma. This transient warming event is inferred to have been marked by a ~7.5 ± 1 °C rise in global mean surface temperature and an increase in atmospheric CO 2 concentrations from ~ 300 -50 +100 to ~700 ± 100 ppm over ~175 kyr during the Late Paleozoic Ice Age. Carbon and mercury isotope records indicate large-scale carbon release and the development of photic-zone euxinia during the KGTM, coincident with a marine biocrisis. The KGTM was initiated by a modest carbon release and temperature increase potentially associated with enhanced volcanic activity at an orbital eccentricity maximum, which likely crossed a climatic tipping point and triggered massive carbon release and abrupt large-scale warming. These findings demonstrate that transient warming events and severe oceanic deterioration can be caused by a modest thermal perturbation within an icehouse climate state analogous to that of the modern Earth.

Abstract Arctic fjords support important, productive ecosystems whose nutrient budgets are highly sensitive to climate-driven deglaciation. Since the influence of nitrification becomes more pronounced as ground thaw progresses, it could sustain primary production as the Arctic growing season lengthens, especially in nitrate-limited fjords. The widespread occurrence of shale bedrock across the Arctic also reveals a likely influence of geogenic nitrogen within the entire region.

This study investigates the petrogenesis of Plio-Quaternary mantle-derived volcanic rocks emplaced across the Iranian Plateau, with a particular focus in determining the role of lithospheric thickness in modulating mantle melting processes. By integrating petrographic observations, whole-rock geochemistry, and Sr-Nd-Pb isotope systematics of 227 samples, we evaluate the possible contributions of asthenospheric and lithospheric mantle, as well as the role of local crust. The results demonstrate that variations in lithospheric thickness, together with mantle temperature, exert a first-order control on the depth and extent of mantle melting, producing systematic differences in trace element ratios, isotopic compositions, and volatile involvement. Mantle-derived magmas from regions underlain by thin lithosphere tend to exhibit Ocean Island Basalt (OIB) like geochemical signatures, a feature that may suggest contributions from pyroxenite-bearing domains within upwelling asthenosphere. Crust assimilation is variably recorded in some volcanic suites from Iran, where trace element systematics deviate toward upper continental crust-like values. The data further suggest that transtensional tectonics and reactivation of strike-slip fault systems played a critical role in facilitating magma upwelling. Collectively, these findings highlight the interplay between lithospheric architecture, mantle temperature, mantle heterogeneity, and tectonic structures in shaping the geochemical diversity of Plio-Quaternary mantle-derived volcanism in Iran, providing new insights into mantle dynamics and intraplate magmatism within collisional orogens.
