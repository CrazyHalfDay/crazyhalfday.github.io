Distinct chalcophile-element inheritance from subducted continental crust in metasomatized mantle

2026-10-21
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Zaicong Wang，Xiang Wang，Haijin Xu，Junbo Zhang，Frances Elaine Jenner 等

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aeh0254

Subduction recycles surface materials into the mantle and shapes its chemical evolution. Whether continental subduction leaves chalcophile-element imprints in the mantle that differ from those of oceanic subduction remains poorly understood. Here, we show that primitive basaltic magmas from the North China Craton exhibit pronounced copper depletion, silver enrichment, and low copper/silver (200 to 1200), together with continental crust–like trace element patterns and radiogenic-isotope compositions. These features contrast sharply with those of the convective mantle, mid-ocean ridge basalts, and oceanic arc basalts, and reflect chemical signatures inherited from subducted continental crust and retained over geological timescales in metasomatized domains of a relatively cold lithospheric mantle. Coupled lead enrichment and unradiogenic lead-isotope signatures further identify metasomatized mantle as an important reservoir of Earth’s unradiogenic lead. Our results reveal distinct modes of chalcophile-element recycling and mantle inheritance during continental and oceanic subduction, with broad implications for mantle chemical evolution and the critical-metal fertility of continental lithosphere.

Structure of a sparsely populated chimeric intermediate that facilitates fold-switching of a metamorphic protein

2026-10-21
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Bodhisatwa Nandi，Ashok Sekhar

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2620010123

Metamorphic proteins challenge the single-sequence-structure-function paradigm by switching between distinct folds that often adopt complementary functions. However, the conformational states sampled along the fold-switching pathway have remained elusive because they are transient and unstable. Using multinuclear chemical exchange saturation transfer and relaxation dispersion NMR, combined with chemical shift–based structure determination and protein engineering, we visualized the atomic resolution structure of an “invisible” state of the metamorphic chemokine lymphotactin. This state is a chimera that integrates the secondary and tertiary structure of one fold with the quaternary assembly of the other. Locking lymphotactin into a single conformation abolishes access to this state, supporting its role as an on-pathway intermediate in metamorphosis. The stabilization of such hybrid intermediates is likely to have facilitated the evolution of fold-switching and represents a powerful strategy for protein design.

Mass-independent oxygen isotope fractionation driven by energetic-particle irradiation of icy solids

2026-10-21
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Gerardo Dominguez，Caroline Caplan，Lauren Tafla，Joshua Lucas，Ming-Chang Liu 等

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2610806123

Mass-independent isotope fractionation (MIF) of oxygen provides key insights into atmospheric chemistry and the origin of isotopic anomalies in planetary materials. To date, MIF has been primarily associated with gas-phase processes involving symmetry-dependent reaction pathways, most notably ozone formation. Whether analogous effects can arise in condensed phases or at interfaces remains an open question. Here we report laboratory experiments demonstrating that energetic particle irradiation of icy oxide surfaces produces mass-independent oxygen isotope fractionation at temperatures as low as 10 K. Electron irradiation of SiO 2 and Al 2 O 3 substrates in the presence of H 2 O ice results in measurable shifts in Δ 17 O in the underlying solids. The resulting three-isotope relationships include slope-1-like behavior in SiO 2 and Al 2 O 3 and distinct, substrate-dependent trends in Al 2 O 3 , indicating that irradiation-induced defect formation and interfacial chemistry govern the isotope effects. The magnitude of the observed anomalies demonstrates that irradiation-driven processes can generate substantial oxygen isotope heterogeneity in condensed phases. These results identify a previously unrecognized class of MIF and provide a quantitative framework for interpreting oxygen isotope signatures in radiation-processed environments, including interstellar grains and early solar system materials.

Effusive sedimentary volcanism experimentally reproduced under Martian-like conditions

2026-10-21
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Ondřej Krýza，Petr Brož，Jacob Adler，Matthew Sylvest，Manish Patel

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04155-x

Abstract Rapid boiling, cooling, and freezing are commonly assumed to rapidly quench metastable liquid eruptions exposed to near-vacuum planetary environments, influencing both sedimentary volcanism on Mars and cryovolcanism on icy worlds. However, how these coupled phase transitions govern eruptive processes remains largely unknown. Here we show experimentally that boiling instead sustains coupled ballistic-effusive mud eruptions under Martian-like pressure conditions (~4.5 mbar). Using an ice-sealed metastable reservoir, we reproduced subsurface-driven eruptions initiated by internally generated boiling-induced overpressure. Eruptions evolved systematically from short-lived ballistic fountains toward episodic effusive emplacement accompanied by coherent mud-flow formation. Ballistic transport redistributed liquid mud around the vent and promoted proximal accumulation that subsequently coalesced into sustained flows despite ongoing cooling and freezing. Numerical modeling further demonstrates that erupted mud would remain largely unfrozen during ballistic transport under Martian gravity conditions. Together, our results show that rapid boiling does not suppress sedimentary volcanism under low-pressure conditions, but instead actively sustains eruptive transport and surface emplacement. These findings provide new constraints on this process on Mars and establish a broader physical framework for metastable liquid eruptions across the Solar System.

2026-10-17
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Arne Lohrberg，Amir Haroon，Nils Moosdorf，Henrik Grob，Emma Hadré 等

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04149-9

Abstract Offshore freshened groundwater (OFG) is a significant yet underexplored component of the global water cycle, and its spatial distribution, particularly its links to past glacial processes, remains poorly constrained. The southeastern North Sea, characterized by extensive tunnel valley networks, provides an ideal setting to test how Pleistocene meltwater shaped offshore groundwater systems. Using newly acquired time-domain controlled-source electromagnetic data integrated with high-resolution seismic reflection surveys, we present a resistivity-based mapping of OFG in this region. The models reveal a laterally continuous OFG body with resistivities of 8–14 Ω·m, extending across multiple stratigraphic units and well beyond individual subglacial landforms. The connection between resistivity anomalies and mapped subglacial landforms shows that the latter can serve as predictive proxies for OFG occurrence. These findings shift the perspective from OFG as isolated anomalies to regionally extensive systems linked to glacial processes, establishing subglacial landforms as a testable screening proxy for locating OFG in formerly glaciated margins worldwide.

2026-10-16
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Zongqi Zou，Shengping Qian，Qiong Wu，Xuran Liang，Fan Yang 等

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aej4446

The global emergence of deep-sea sediments enriched in rare-earth elements and yttrium (REY) after the Late Eocene cooling coincides spatiotemporally with Antarctic bottom water (AABW) expansion, yet the underlying mechanism remains unresolved. Here, we present in situ elemental and neodymium isotopic analyses of apatites extracted from Oligocene-Miocene sediments along the North Pacific AABW pathway. Results show ∼90% of the REY budget resides in inner dentin of fossil teeth. We propose that AABW collects, transports, and sorts fossil teeth along its flow path, ultimately leading to the global occurrence of REY-rich sediments in downstream basins.

From fragmentation to fault zone architecture as a structural framework for fault weakening

从碎裂到断层带结构：作为断层弱化的构造框架

2026-10-16
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Nathalie Casas，Guilhem Mollon，Marco Maria Scuderi

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78254-w

Earthquake nucleation is governed by the evolution of fault zone structure, yet the physical mechanisms linking grain-scale processes to macroscopic weakening remain elusive. Here we use discrete element simulations of quartz-rich gouge in which angular grains fragment dynamically, revealing a robust strain–damage law in which damage accumulates as grains are consumed. Grain size dictates the geometry and timing of localization, while fragmentation enhances compaction and promotes the emergence of a dominant Principal Slip Zone (PSZ). As comminution approaches a saturation state (i.e. minimum grain size), localization can migrate to competing bands, providing a mechanical explanation for overprinted shear fabrics in experiments and natural faults. By linking fragmentation, porosity evolution, and the resulting shear localization, our results provide a local fragmentation state variable for constitutive laws that couple grain-size evolution to fault weakening and the conditions for earthquake nucleation. This study combines simulations and laboratory data to show how grains breaking inside fault gouge reshape fault-zone architecture, focus slip into narrow bands and help faults weaken during deformation.
