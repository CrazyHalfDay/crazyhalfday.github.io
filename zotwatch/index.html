Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78601-x

Using astrochronology, Wang et al. estimate that relative sea-level change led benthic turnover by ~70,000 years during the late Cambrian SPICE event under their age model, supporting links among orbital forcing, marine anoxia and biodiversity loss. The prominent Cambrian Steptoean Positive Carbon Isotope Excursion (SPICE) stands as one of Earth’s largest carbon cycle perturbations, marked by widespread marine anoxia, climatic upheaval, and biotic turnover, yet the complete extent and interplay of these environmental changes remain unclear. Here, we integrate high-resolution astrochronology, carbonate carbon isotope records from the North China Block and marine species richness to investigate sedimentary and biogeochemical responses during the SPICE interval. Time series analysis reveals a distinct ~2-million-year sedimentary rhythm that aligns with cycles in reconstructed sea-level fluctuations and biodiversity dynamics. We show that sea-level changes preceded shifts in benthic species richness by 70 kyr, linking astronomically paced eustatic oscillations to marine redox fluctuations and concurrent biodiversity turnover. These findings establish pivotal constraints on ocean-climate interactions during this critical interval, elucidating Earth system resilience to extreme climatic and biogeochemical perturbations.

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04081-y

Abstract Sediment respiration in streams/rivers impacts global biogeochemical cycles, yet is mostly studied under perennially-inundated conditions despite all river corridors having variably inundated areas and most streams going dry each year. Previous experiments observed sediment rewetting to cause elevated or suppressed aerobic respiration, indicating variation across studies. To deepen understanding, we conducted 21-day wet/dry experiments with sediments from 64 streams spread across the contiguous United States. Here we show that drying and rewetting sediments from perennial and non-perennial streams had near-neutral effects or suppressed O 2 consumption rates. These biogeochemical cold moments contrast previous studies that observed hot moments. Sediment texture and microbial activity explained variation in responses to rewetting, but this did not reveal why some studies observed cold moments while others observed hot moments. To solve this we propose a unifying conceptual model whereby hot and cold moments exist simultaneously upon rewetting by considering both dry-state and perennially-inundated reference points.

2026-10-23
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Huan Liu，Kaushik Bhattacharya

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2614478123

Molluscan shells come in various shapes and sizes. Despite this diversity, each species produces a shell with a characteristic shape that is independent of environmental conditions. We seek to understand this robust complexity. We are guided by two principles in the spirit of D’Arcy Thompson. First, the growth is governed by the repeated and continuous application of a fixed growth law, even as the shell evolves in overall shape, without any complex biological machinery to monitor and control the growth. Second, the growth law depends solely on local geometry at the shell’s growing edge. The first principle naturally leads to the mathematical statement that the shape of the shell is generated by the action of a Lie group on a protoconch. The second naturally leads to a particular representation of the Lie group. We relate these parameters to the phylogenetic tree. In addition to the morphogenetic insight, our results potentially point to a alternative growth-based approach to engineering complex structures.

Abstract Enhanced weathering, adding crushed silicate minerals to soils, is being explored as a way to draw down atmospheric carbon dioxide, but its effects on soil organic carbon remain uncertain. Adding olivine with glucose initially suppresses microbial carbon uptake, but by week 55 instead boosts microbial biomass and mineral-associated organic matter more than glucose alone, while lowering respiratory carbon loss. Our findings suggest that when labile carbon is available, enhanced weathering can increase soil carbon storage primarily through microbial processing, rather than by creating new mineral surfaces for carbon to bind to.

Abstract Nutrient enrichment and warming cause widespread costal deoxygenation, leading to hypoxia or even anoxia in bottom waters. Effusive sedimentary volcanism experimentally reproduced under Martian-like conditions

2026-10-21
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Ondřej Krýza，Petr Brož，Jacob Adler，Matthew Sylvest，Manish Patel

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04155-x

Abstract Rapid boiling, cooling, and freezing are commonly assumed to rapidly quench metastable liquid eruptions exposed to near-vacuum planetary environments, influencing both sedimentary volcanism on Mars and cryovolcanism on icy worlds. However, how these coupled phase transitions govern eruptive processes remains largely unknown. Here we show experimentally that boiling instead sustains coupled ballistic-effusive mud eruptions under Martian-like pressure conditions (~4.5 mbar). Using an ice-sealed metastable reservoir, we reproduced subsurface-driven eruptions initiated by internally generated boiling-induced overpressure. Eruptions evolved systematically from short-lived ballistic fountains toward episodic effusive emplacement accompanied by coherent mud-flow formation. Ballistic transport redistributed liquid mud around the vent and promoted proximal accumulation that subsequently coalesced into sustained flows despite ongoing cooling and freezing. Numerical modeling further demonstrates that erupted mud would remain largely unfrozen during ballistic transport under Martian gravity conditions. Together, our results show that rapid boiling does not suppress sedimentary volcanism under low-pressure conditions, but instead actively sustains eruptive transport and surface emplacement. These findings provide new constraints on this process on Mars and establish a broader physical framework for metastable liquid eruptions across the Solar System.

Our model elucidates the critical magmatic-fluid processes controlling the formation of large-scale Li deposits in South China.
