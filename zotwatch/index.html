Subglacial landforms as proxy for offshore freshened groundwater in formerly glaciated margins

2026-10-17
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Arne Lohrberg，Amir Haroon，Nils Moosdorf，Henrik Grob，Emma Hadré 等

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04149-9

Abstract Offshore freshened groundwater (OFG) is a significant yet underexplored component of the global water cycle, and its spatial distribution, particularly its links to past glacial processes, remains poorly constrained. The southeastern North Sea, characterized by extensive tunnel valley networks, provides an ideal setting to test how Pleistocene meltwater shaped offshore groundwater systems. Using newly acquired time-domain controlled-source electromagnetic data integrated with high-resolution seismic reflection surveys, we present a resistivity-based mapping of OFG in this region. The models reveal a laterally continuous OFG body with resistivities of 8–14 Ω·m, extending across multiple stratigraphic units and well beyond individual subglacial landforms. This configuration is best explained by subglacial meltwater flushing of sediment porewaters during Pleistocene glaciations. Subsequent preservation was aided by density contrasts that limit vertical mixing and by fine-grained glaciomarine deposits sealing the system from above. The connection between resistivity anomalies and mapped subglacial landforms shows that the latter can serve as predictive proxies for OFG occurrence. These findings shift the perspective from OFG as isolated anomalies to regionally extensive systems linked to glacial processes, establishing subglacial landforms as a testable screening proxy for locating OFG in formerly glaciated margins worldwide.

Permeability heterogeneity controls fluid migration and microseismicity evolution in The Geysers geothermal field, California

2026-10-17
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Lei Li，Xiaobao Zeng，Ian W. McBrearty，Xin Cui，Xing Tan 等

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78210-8

Continuous seismic observations and numerical simulations reveal that, over the past decade, fluid migration and microseismic responses at The Geysers geothermal field have been primarily controlled by heterogeneous permeability. Fracture networks directly govern the efficiency of geothermal energy extraction and play a critical role in fluid migration and microearthquake generation. Using deep learning and Markov clustering, we build a high-resolution microseismic catalog covering the past decade for The Geysers geothermal field, California. Our observations reveal distinctive clustering patterns and inferred fracture systems tightly correlated with injection operations. Source mechanism analyses and pore pressure simulations indicate that the inferred connected fracture network provides pathways for rapid and directional pore pressure diffusion, highlighting that heterogeneous permeability controls the localized migration of fluids and the associated microseismic response. Our study highlights the importance of continuous and integrated seismic monitoring for ensuring the sustainability of long-term reservoir development and exploitation.

Dual Nd isotopic archives in fossil teeth reveal bottom-current control of rare-earth element enrichment in deep-sea sediments

2026-10-16
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Zongqi Zou，Shengping Qian，Qiong Wu，Xuran Liang，Fan Yang 等

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aej4446

The global emergence of deep-sea sediments enriched in rare-earth elements and yttrium (REY) after the Late Eocene cooling coincides spatiotemporally with Antarctic bottom water (AABW) expansion, yet the underlying mechanism remains unresolved. Here, we present in situ elemental and neodymium isotopic analyses of apatites extracted from Oligocene-Miocene sediments along the North Pacific AABW pathway. Results show ∼90% of the REY budget resides in inner dentin of fossil teeth. The dentin and coexisting authigenic fluorapatites display similar ε Nd values (∼−5) matching North Pacific seawater, whereas outer dense layer carries a distinct, less-radiogenic ε Nd (∼−10) resembling South Pacific seawater. This isotopic decoupling establishes fossil teeth as dual archives: The outer layer preserves the ε Nd signature of the fish’s living environment, while the inner dentin records its depositional environment. We propose that AABW collects, transports, and sorts fossil teeth along its flow path, ultimately leading to the global occurrence of REY-rich sediments in downstream basins.

A comprehensive phylogenomic framework for brachiopod evolution among lophotrochozoans

2026-10-16
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Mingzhu Yang，Aodhán Butler，Natalia López Carranza，Jakob Vinther，Rowan Whittle 等

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aeh5363

Brachiopods appeared early in the Cambrian radiation and represent an important clade for understanding the origin of animal body plans and the evolution of biomineralized structures. A robust phylogenetic framework of the living representatives is needed to answer persistent questions about the evolution of brachiopod mineralogy, shell morphology, and identification of the brachiopod sister group among Lophotrochozoa. To the best of our knowledge, we present the first phylogenomic analysis of Brachiopoda and related lophotrochozoan lineages using a comprehensive dataset encompassing all five extant brachiopod orders and using a custom-developed data-refining strategy, compositionally heterogeneous substitution models, and a novel implementation of the parametric-bootstrap likelihood ratio test. We recover monophyly of Lophophorata, with strong support for Brachiopoda as sister to Bryozoa plus Phoronida. Within Brachiopoda, we recover the clade of inarticulated phosphatic Linguliformea sister to the inarticulated calcitic Craniiformea, with both sister to the articulated calcitic Rhynchonellata, with miniaturized Thecideida unexpectedly nested within Terebratulida. Our phylogeny implies that at least one evolutionary transition in primary mineralogy occurred in Brachiopoda, a phenomenon not recognized in any other metazoan group.

From fragmentation to fault zone architecture as a structural framework for fault weakening

2026-10-16
Nature Communications (IF: 14.7)

Nathalie Casas，Guilhem Mollon，Marco Maria Scuderi

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78254-w

Earthquake nucleation is governed by the evolution of fault zone structure, yet the physical mechanisms linking grain-scale processes to macroscopic weakening remain elusive. Here we use discrete element simulations of quartz-rich gouge in which angular grains fragment dynamically, revealing a robust strain–damage law in which damage accumulates as grains are consumed. Grain size dictates the geometry and timing of localization, while fragmentation enhances compaction and promotes the emergence of a dominant Principal Slip Zone (PSZ). As comminution approaches a saturation state (i.e. minimum grain size), localization can migrate to competing bands, providing a mechanical explanation for overprinted shear fabrics in experiments and natural faults. By linking fragmentation, porosity evolution, and the resulting shear localization, our results provide a local fragmentation state variable for constitutive laws that couple grain-size evolution to fault weakening and the conditions for earthquake nucleation. This study combines simulations and laboratory data to show how grains breaking inside fault gouge reshape fault-zone architecture, focus slip into narrow bands and help faults weaken during deformation.

2026-10-15
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Ke Zhu，Maosheng Yang，Jun Huang，Wei Tian，Meng-Hua Zhu 等

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04139-x

Abstract Micrometeorite bombardment is a fundamental surface process on the Moon and other airless bodies, driving elemental evaporation, isotopic fractionation, and material mixing in surface regoliths. Here, we report high-precision stable Ni isotope data for lunar soils returned by the Apollo (11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17) and Chang’e (5 and 6) missions, covering geographically and geologically diverse lunar terrains. The analyzed soils show δ 60/58 Ni values of 0.345‰ to 1.280‰ (relative to NIST SRM 986), generally heavier than lunar basalts and the inferred bulk silicate Moon, indicating widespread heavy Ni isotope enrichment during surface processing. Together with published Chang’e-5 drill-core data, these results demonstrate that micrometeorite-driven Ni isotope fractionation is widespread across the lunar surface. Local variability, however, reflects additional controls. The relatively low δ 60/58 Ni values of the immature Apollo 12 soils are consistent with limited cumulative micrometeorite processing, whereas the mature Apollo 16 soils have high Ni concentrations and Ni/Co ratios, indicating additional compositional overprinting by Ni-rich material. Stable Ni isotopes, particularly when combined with regolith maturity and Ni/Co ratios, therefore provide a sensitive tracer of micrometeorite-driven lunar surface processing and its local overprinting.

In Lake Iseo, Italy, human-driven erosion amplified sediment accumulation and preconditioned the delta for catastrophic earthquake-induced collapses and tsunamis, revealing a global mechanism linking human land use to increasing tsunami hazards in lakes. Abstract Ultramafic rocks are a global resource with vast potential for permanent CO 2 mineralization. Abstract The 2025 M 7.8 Mandalay earthquake ruptured ~480 km of the Sagaing Fault, producing one of the longest strike-slip events documented and impacting a vulnerable region. Our approach can inform rupture scenarios in understudied regions.
