Weathering-derived calcium supports carbon storage in grassland soils

风化来源的钙支撑草地土壤碳储存

2026-10-13
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

E. W. Slessarev，H. R. Goertzen，R. A. Lybrand，K. J. McFarlane，J. Pett-Ridge 等

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04142-2

Abstract Dark colored grassland soils, known as Mollisols, are intensively farmed and rich in organic matter, and hence have an important role in the carbon cycle. Organic carbon storage in Mollisols may result from interactions between organic matter and calcium released by carbonate and silicate weathering. Here we test this hypothesis by using a geochemical model to simulate calcium release from weathering in soils across the USA. We find that calcium released by mineral weathering is associated with soil organic carbon storage and that this effect is stronger in Mollisols than in non-Mollisols. Our model also shows that agriculture has increased calcium inputs to cultivated USA Mollisols 4-5 fold, indicating that humans have fundamentally changed the geochemical conditions that sustain organic carbon in these soils. By implication, moderating the quantity of and timing of calcium release may help to preserve and restore carbon in the world’s most intensively farmed landscapes.

Phylogenomics confirms Palaeoptera monophyly and illuminates the origin of winged insects

2026-10-12
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Shiyu Du，Yehao Wang，Feng Zhang，Michael S. Engel，Xuhongyi Zheng 等

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2614706123

The origin and early radiation of winged insects (Pterygota) represent one of the most transformative events in terrestrial evolution, yet phylogenetic relationships among their basalmost lineages (Ephemeroptera, Odonata and Neoptera) continue to be a central debate within insect systematics. Here, we present the most taxonomically comprehensive phylogenomic analysis to date, leveraging genomic and transcriptomic data from 101 species, including 16 sequenced ephemeropteran genomes. Employing 1,367 universal single-copy orthologs across two supermatrices, we used concatenation- and coalescent-based frameworks under homogeneous and heterogeneous, and multispecies coalescent models. Our analyses support Palaeoptera monophyly. Although coalescent-based methods initially suggested a Chiastomyaria (Ephemeroptera + Neoptera) topology, subsequent signal dissection and topology tests confirm that the preponderance of genomic signal favors a monophyletic Palaeoptera. Furthermore, we identify incomplete lineage sorting and gene tree estimation error as the primary drivers of previous topological conflicts. Our molecular dating results point to an Early Devonian origin of crown-Pterygota, whereas crown-Palaeoptera first emerged in the Late Devonian. By reconciling molecular evidence with morphological synapomorphies, such as wing-base sclerite homologies, this study provides a coherent framework for the early evolution of winged insects, supports a single origin of wings, and identifies the neopteran wing-folding mechanism as a singular evolutionary innovation.

Physics-informed deep learning links geodetic data and fault friction

2026-10-12
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Rikuto Fukushima，Masayuki Kano，Kazuro Hirahara，Makiko Ohtani

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04138-y

Fault slip modeling, based on laboratory-derived friction laws, has significantly enhanced our understanding of fault mechanics. Agreement between model predictions and observations supports the hypothesis that observed slip diversity, including fast earthquakes and slow transient slips (Slow Slip Events; SSEs), originates from frictional heterogeneity. However, quantitative assessments of frictional heterogeneity from geodetic observations while fully incorporating fault mechanics are lacking due to the difficulties of high-dimensional optimization. In this study, we aim to address this gap using Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to link frictional heterogeneity with geodetic observations. PINNs employ a neural network to represent the spatially variable frictional properties, making their estimation feasible. Targeting the 2010 Bungo SSE in southwest Japan, our estimation reveals heterogeneous friction coinciding with localized SSE nucleation in southwest Shikoku, and subsequent westward propagation. The calculated fault slip of SSE successfully reproduces the spatio-temporal pattern of observed surface displacements. This PINN-based inversion provides a mechanically consistent fault slip model validated through quantitative comparison with observations. Furthermore, we forecast the future fault slip evolution, demonstrating the importance of assimilating observations spanning multiple SSE cycles. Our results demonstrate the potential of PINN for advancing understanding of fault mechanics and enabling physics-based fault slip forecasting. Spatially variable frictional properties along a fault can be estimated using a physics-informed neural network approach that enables physics-consistent slip modelling, as tested on the 2010 Bungo slow slip event in southwest Japan.

Abstract Marine coastal systems are important for global biogeochemical cycling and are particularly sensitive to climate change effects due to their shallow depths, which results in rapid heat transfer to the sediments. Hydrogen isotopes reveal water leakage from the core

2026-10-09
Science Advances (IF: 11.7)

Yu Zhang，Wenzhong Wang，Zhongqing Wu，Bowen Chen

DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aei4756

Chemical heterogeneities in Earth’s deep mantle may record either preserved primordial reservoirs or later core-mantle exchange, but distinguishing between these origins remains challenging. Hydrogen isotopes offer a sensitive tracer, yet whether magma ocean crystallization could generate a deuterium-depleted deep reservoir has not been quantitatively evaluated. Here, we use machine learning–accelerated path-integral simulations to determine equilibrium hydrogen isotope fractionation between silicate melt and bridgmanite, ringwoodite, and wadsleyite under magma ocean conditions. Incorporating these fractionation factors into a magma ocean crystallization model shows that mineral-melt fractionation is intrinsically weak, producing an essentially homogeneous D/H distribution in the primitive mantle. Magma ocean crystallization therefore cannot generate the extremely low δD values observed in some ocean island basalts. Combined with exceptionally high 3 He/ 4 He ratios, these signatures more plausibly reflect selective transfer of primordial volatiles from Earth’s core, implying sustained volatile exchange across the core-mantle boundary.

The land-to-ocean export of carbon via rivers is a key component in the global carbon cycle. In this Review, we provide an overview of the factors governing the mobilization of carbon into rivers and those modulating riverine carbon export to the ocean. Terrestrial landscapes are estimated to deliver 3–4 PgC yr^−1 to inland waters, with the majority outgassed rather than exported to the ocean. Integrating these large datasets with mechanistic studies of biogeochemical modulators and machine learning approaches would inform understanding of carbon dynamics within major watersheds and improve predictions of this globally important carbon flux. Globally, rivers export ~10^15 grams of carbon to the ocean each year. This Review explores the mobilization, modulation and transport of different species of carbon within watersheds and rivers, with the aim to help inform and improve land-to-ocean carbon flux estimates.

A unified magmatic-hydrothermal model for the IOA-IOCG continuum: Integrating geological, geochemical, quantitative, and physicochemical constraints

2026-10-13

Ahmed S. Moftah，Fangyue Wang，M.H. Salama，Geng Tao，Long Zhang

DOI: 10.1016/j.oregeorev.2026.107594

Iron oxide-apatite (IOA) and iron oxide-copper–gold (IOCG) deposits are economically important ore systems, but their genetic relationship remains controversial. This review presents a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed IOA-IOCG continuum by integrating multidisciplinary geological, mineralogical, textural, geochemical, isotopic, mineral-chemical, quantitative statistical, and physicochemical evidence to develop a unified magmatic-hydrothermal model and evaluate whether IOA and IOCG deposits represent genetically related stages within a single evolving magmatic-hydrothermal system. Magnetite from both IOA and IOCG deposits records magmatic-hydrothermal crystallization, whereas actinolite compositions indicate comparable physicochemical conditions. Multivariate statistical analyses further demonstrate that magnetite compositions occupy statistically distinguishable but partially overlapping compositional domains, with systematic trace-element variations supporting a shared magmatic-hydrothermal origin followed by divergent physicochemical and paragenetic evolution. Apatite records hydrothermal signatures in both IOA and IOCG deposits, whereas quantitative statistical analyses reveal systematic differences in volatile (Cl-S) compositions and REE-pattern geometries, indicating distinct fluid evolution and physicochemical histories within a shared magmatic-hydrothermal system. The IOA and IOCG deposits display closely similar δ56Fe and δ18O values. These multiple lines of evidence support a genetic link between the two mineralization styles developed in subduction-related continental arc settings. In this study, we propose a multi-stage genetic model in which IOA mineralization initially forms at depth from Fe-rich magmas within lower-crustal MASH zones, followed by IOCG formation through interaction between magmatic-hydrothermal fluids and externally derived brines at shallower crustal levels. A late-stage, shallow IOA event may occur due to metal depletion or evolving fluid conditions, reflecting a cyclic or pulsed mineralizing system. This model aligns with observations from extensively studied deposits, including El Laco in Chile.

Lithos (IF: 4.5)

DOI: 10.1016/j.lithos.2026.108817
