Mechanoradical hydrogen generation during seismic slip in an active fault zone

活动断裂带地震滑动过程中机械自由基产氢

2026-08-10
Communications Earth &amp; Environment (IF: 8.1)

Yun Ya Ling，Li-Wei Kuo，Kuo-Fong Ma，Wen-Jie Wu

DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03912-2

Black fault gouge identified in the rupture zone of the 2018 Hualien earthquake, Taiwan, bears abundant CaO and organic matter together with frictional melt, suggesting significant shear heating and natural hydrogen generation via mechanoradical reactions. Laboratory experiments and gas measurements suggest that rapid shearing in fault zones can trigger mechanoradical reactions that dissociate water and produce natural hydrogen. However, the lack of direct evidence means it is unclear whether this process operates in nature. We present mineralogical and microstructural observations of fault gouges recovered from the Milun Fault core three years after the 2018 M_W 6.4 Hualien earthquake in Taiwan. Quartz-rich yellow gouge contains clay and calcite but lacks carbonaceous material (CM). In contrast, black gouge contains crystallized frictional melt together with lime (CaO) and CM, suggesting that silicate minerals and calcite underwent shear heating during seismic slip. The formation of CM is experimentally associated with hydrogen-rich conditions, whereas lime is unstable in the presence of water. Therefore, the coexistence of lime and CM suggests formation under hydrogen-bearing and water-depleted conditions, consistent with mechanoradical reactions. Our results provide evidence of decarbonation and possible mechanoradical hydrogen generation during shearing in natural fault zones.

A transient global warming event during Earth’s penultimate icehouse

地球倒数第二次冰室期间的一次短暂全球变暖事件

2026-08-10
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (IF: 11.1)

Le Yao，Thomas J. Algeo，Qiulai Wang，Wang Zheng，Qiang Wei 等

DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2601643123

Deep-time hyperthermal/transient warming events provide critical analogs for modern climatic warming and its impacts. However, all ancient hyperthermals documented to date have occurred under greenhouse climate states, limiting their relevance for understanding the trajectory of present-day climate change. Here, we present conodont oxygen isotope data and climate modeling for the Kasimovian–Gzhelian Thermal Maximum (KGTM) at ~304 Ma. This transient warming event is inferred to have been marked by a ~7.5 ± 1 °C rise in global mean surface temperature and an increase in atmospheric CO 2 concentrations from ~ 300 -50 +100 to ~700 ± 100 ppm over ~175 kyr during the Late Paleozoic Ice Age. Carbon and mercury isotope records indicate large-scale carbon release and the development of photic-zone euxinia during the KGTM, coincident with a marine biocrisis. The KGTM was initiated by a modest carbon release and temperature increase potentially associated with enhanced volcanic activity at an orbital eccentricity maximum, which likely crossed a climatic tipping point and triggered massive carbon release and abrupt large-scale warming. These findings demonstrate that transient warming events and severe oceanic deterioration can be caused by a modest thermal perturbation within an icehouse climate state analogous to that of the modern Earth.

Abstract Arctic fjords support important, productive ecosystems whose nutrient budgets are highly sensitive to climate-driven deglaciation. Here we present over three decades of data describing the nitrogen biogeochemistry of streamflow in the Kongsfjord and Adventfjord regions (West Spitsbergen, Svalbard) to show that microbial nitrate production by nitrification can substantially increase nitrate export to these fjords by runoff. Stable isotopes show that the nitrification of rock or “geogenic” nitrogen is particularly important, especially where shale-rich sedimentary rocks are present. In these cases, the release of nitrified nitrate into runoff may increase total riverine nitrate export by up to ~25 times, providing a potentially significant resource for utilization by downstream ecosystems. Since the influence of nitrification becomes more pronounced as ground thaw progresses, it could sustain primary production as the Arctic growing season lengthens, especially in nitrate-limited fjords. The widespread occurrence of shale bedrock across the Arctic also reveals a likely influence of geogenic nitrogen within the entire region.

IIIE irons share geochemical characteristics with howardite-eucrite-diogenite meteorites suggesting both may have formed from differentiated planetesimal parent bodies, according to analyses on a silicate-bearing inclusion in the Aletai IIIE iron meteorite. Rocky planets formed through the differentiation of planetesimals into metal‑rich cores and silicate mantles, yet direct evidence linking these complementary reservoirs remains scarce. Here we show that IIIE irons and howardite-eucrite-diogenite (HED) meteorites are genetically linked, by integrating mineralogical, isotopic, and rare earth element (REE) analyses of the silicate-bearing Aletai IIIE meteorite. A pronounced negative Eu anomaly in orthopyroxene marks the onset of plagioclase saturation and buoyant segregation during early magma ocean crystallization, supported by thermodynamic modelling. These findings establish a genetic link between magmatic irons and differentiated achondrites, suggesting that IIIE irons and HEDs share a common genetic heritage, and providing direct physical evidence for core-mantle reservoir connections in the early Solar System.

Abstract Alternation of magmatic and amagmatic seafloor along the slow and ultraslow spreading ridges is common. Yet, temporal variation is obscured by sediments blanketing oceanic basement away from spreading centers. The economically large Jinchuan NiCu sulfide deposit of Northwest China consists of distinct eastern and western intrusions, whose primary magmatic signatures have been heavily erased by pervasive hydrothermal overprint. Although alteration-resistant apatite effectively incorporates trace elements and serves as a reliable petrogenetic-metallogenic tracer, systematic apatite geochemical contrasts recording divergent magmatic-mineralization evolution between the two intrusions remain poorly constrained. We integrate major and trace element compositions and SrNd isotopes of apatite from major lithologic layers in the eastern and western intrusions to constrain magma evolution and ore-forming processes. Mineralogical observations reveal marked differences in grain morphology, paragenesis, and textural occurrence of apatite in disseminated, patchy, and net-textured sulfides from these two intrusions (abbreviated as E-DS, E-PTS, E-NTS, W-DS, and W-NTS Ap). The eastern intrusion contains magmatic fluorapatites (E-DS and E-PTS Ap) and chlorapatite (E-NTS Ap), whereas the western intrusion exclusively hosts chlorapatite (W-DS and W-NTS Ap), almost all apatite exhibiting LREE enrichment and HREE depletion. Apatite SrNd isotope modelling indicates contamination of <20% of deep crust and <10% of marble country rocks, yet the western intrusion underwent intense localized marble assimilation. Elevated Th and Zr abundances and Pb/Ce ratios in apatite from the eastern intrusion suggest a more pronounced deep crustal assimilation. Apatite Mn and V contents, coupled with Eu/Eu* and Ce/Ce* ratios as well as S+6 substitution mechanisms, demonstrate a systematic decrease in magmatic fO2 from NTS to DS and PTS ores, with the eastern intrusion showing higher fO2 than the western intrusion. Stronger deep crustal assimilation and elevated magmatic fO2 in the eastern intrusion correlate with larger metal endowments, indicating that deep sulfur-bearing crustal contamination and sulfide melt segregation dominate ore formation. The Sr/Y, La/Yb, and Eu/Gd ratios of apatite reveal plagioclase-dominated crystallization in the eastern intrusion versus clinopyroxene–amphibole fractionation in the western intrusion, with their distinct evolutionary paths governed by crystallization depth. Modelling of F-Cl-OH compositions suggests higher initial volatile contents in the western magma compared to the eastern magma. The magmatic H2O contents of ~2.5 wt% significantly enhanced the magma's maximum carrying capacity of sulfide melts to ~10 vol%, facilitating sulfide accumulation and enrichment. The primitive magma Cl/F ratios exceeding 10 suggest that the Jinchuan deep mantle source was metasomatized by Cl-rich slab fluids. We propose that the formation of the Jinchuan primary magma resulted from the interactive melting and hybridization of a hot mantle plume and metasomatized lithospheric mantle. This work highlights the utility of integrated apatite geochemical proxies in tracking magmatic evolution and ore-forming processes in the Jinchuan deposit, with broader implications for the study of global magmatic NiCu sulfide deposits.

The C3-SI framework: A Newtonian model for compositional-contrast-controlled subduction initiation

Yaoling Niu，Yanhong Chen，Pu Sun，Hui Huang，Fangyu Shen

DOI: 10.1016/j.gsf.2026.102427

Subduction initiation (SI) remains one of the most fundamental unresolved problems in solid Earth geodynamics. Building on the hypothesis of Niu et al. (2003) that SI requires lateral compositional buoyancy contrasts within the lithosphere, we develop here the first fully quantitative C3–SI (Compositional-Contrast-Controlled Subduction Initiation) framework based on transparent Newtonian mechanics. The framework integrates two components: (1) the Subduction Initiation Index (SII), which quantifies lithospheric rupture driven by compositional buoyancy contrast, and (2) the Fault-Block Sequential Sinking (FBSS) mechanism, which explains the transition from rupture to self-sustaining subduction. We show that ocean-continental (O–C) and ocean-oceanic plateau (O–P) boundaries generate lateral horizontal buoyancy compressive stresses ( σ O - C ∼ 120 MPa and σ O - P ∼ 60 MPa, respectively), far exceeding ridge push ( σ R P ≈ 22 MPa). This stress-equivalent term represents the horizontal compressive stress generated by the lateral gravitational potential energy (GPE) gradient, analogous to ridge push. These stresses are strongly localized within ∼50 km of the boundary and decay rapidly toward plate interiors, explaining why SI is spatially restricted. The SII successfully predicts where rupture occurs, but rupture alone cannot produce subduction. Lithosphere-scale bending is mechanically implausible because required stresses (∼1–3 GPa) exceed available tectonic stresses by an order of magnitude. The FBSS resolves this by treating the oceanic lithosphere as a fault-segmented system, where inherited abyssal-hill faults enable sequential block sinking. As a 100-km-wide slab segment sinks to ∼50–60 km into the asthenosphere, slab-pull reaches a threshold of ∼3 × 1018 N, driving acceleration to >50 mm/yr and enabling self-sustaining subduction. The predicted spatial localization, force balance, and timescales (1–3 Myr) are consistent with global observations. The C3–SI framework provides the first fully Newtonian and transparent mechanical description of SI, resolving the long-standing problem of how lithospheric rupture occurs and how rupture evolves into subduction, without invoking ad hoc weakening mechanisms in opaque numerical models. A global survey of ∼30 subduction zones (50 segments) confirms that most SI events occur at O–C or O–P boundaries. Even the so-called intra-oceanic systems (e.g., Mariana and Tonga) are underlain by compositionally highly depleted, physically buoyant forearc lithospheric mantle, consistent with the original hypothesis of Niu et al. (2003).
